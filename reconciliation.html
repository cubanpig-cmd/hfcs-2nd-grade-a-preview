First Reconciliation

Getting ready for confession

A practice page for Holy Family 2nd Grade A. The teaching is ours. It is not taken from a workbook.

The Act of Contrition on this page is the one from the Order of Penance, said in confession. Start with Must know.

What this sacrament is

God is holy and loves you. Jesus is the Good Shepherd. He watches over you like a shepherd watches sheep.

Sometimes we choose to turn away. That choice is called sin. Reconciliation is how Jesus welcomes us back.

You tell a priest your sins. He listens in Jesus’ name. God forgives you. The priest cannot tell anyone what you said.

Parents: nothing here is copied from a textbook. Memorize the Act of Contrition your teacher gave you. Use Must know to practice the ideas, the commandments, and the words of confession.

1

God loves you

At home

Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession.

There is one God. He is Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Three Persons, one God. We call that the Trinity. Nobody can explain it all. We believe it because God told us.

The Father made the world and loves you. The Holy Spirit is the helper who came to live in you at Baptism. Jesus is the Son. He called himself the Good Shepherd. A good shepherd knows each sheep, leads them, and would rather be hurt himself than lose one.

People who love Jesus still make bad choices. Pretending we never do is a lie. Telling God the truth is how we let him bring us home.

Saint Damien left a comfortable life to care for sick people others avoided. He kept asking God for mercy, even when he was sick himself. Saints are not people who never failed. They are people who came back.

2

Rules that show love

At home

Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession.

Long ago God gave Moses ten rules. They are not a trap. They show how to love God and how to love people. Jesus later said two loves hold all ten: love God with your whole heart, and love your neighbor as yourself.

Read each commandment below in Church words, then in second-grade words. The questions are for a quiet talk with a parent. You do not have to say private answers out loud.

Saint Dominic Savio was a boy who loved Mass. When children around him started to fight, he asked them to stop for Jesus and to make peace. Holiness can look like stopping a fight.

  1. 1. I am the Lord your God. You shall not have other gods before me.

    Love God first. Do not let toys, money, or fame be more important than God.

  2. 2. You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain.

    Say God’s name with love, never as a joke or a curse.

  3. 3. Remember to keep holy the Lord’s day.

    Sunday is God’s day. Go to Mass with a willing heart.

  4. 4. Honor your father and mother.

    Listen to your parents, grandparents, and teachers.

  5. 5. You shall not kill.

    Do not hurt people with hits or with mean words.

  6. 6. You shall not commit adultery.

    When people marry, God wants them to love and stay faithful.

  7. 7. You shall not steal.

    Do not take what is not yours.

  8. 8. You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor.

    Tell the truth. Do not blame someone else.

  9. 9. You shall not covet your neighbor’s wife.

    Be glad for other families. Do not wish you had someone else’s husband or wife.

  10. 10. You shall not covet your neighbor’s goods.

    Be glad for what you have. Do not stew over a friend’s bike or game.

Jesus’ two great commandments

1. Love the Lord your God with all your heart.

2. Love your neighbor as yourself.

They cover the other ten completely, like an envelope covers a letter.

3

You can choose

At home

Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession.

You can think and you can choose. That freedom is a gift. God created us to know him, to love him, and to serve him in this world, and to be happy with him forever in heaven. Love does not count if someone forces it. God will not force you to love him.

A sin is a wrong you choose with your words, your actions, or your wants. A spill, a forgetful moment, or something you did not understand is not a sin.

  • Original sin is the broken start of the human family. We are born needing a Savior. Mary was prepared by God. Jesus is God the Son.
  • A smaller sin, called venial, bruises the friendship. It does not end it.
  • A mortal sin is serious, you know it is serious, and you choose it anyway. It breaks the friendship. God can still forgive it.

The candle on this page is our picture, not a craft from a book. The flame means God’s life in you. Bad choices make it struggle. A mortal sin puts it out. Confession lights it again. God’s love never goes out, even when our flame does.

Saint Maria Goretti was young. She refused to join in a serious sin, and she forgave the person who hurt her. Her story is sad. The part to keep is this: she chose God, and mercy can change a heart.

Each wrong choice makes it harder to see. Tap a choice. Then ask forgiveness.

Seven signs of God’s help

At home

Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession. If you still have Baptism photos, a gown, or a candle, show them and say, “That day you became God’s child.”

Jesus gave the Church seven sacraments. A sacrament is a sign we can see, hear, or touch. Through it, God gives grace, which is his own life and help.

  • Baptism
  • Reconciliation
  • Eucharist
  • Confirmation
  • Matrimony
  • Holy Orders
  • Anointing of the Sick

They make us holy, they build up the Church, and they give God worship. Baptism is once. It washes original sin and any personal sin, and it makes you part of the Church. This year you are getting ready for two more: Reconciliation, and then the Eucharist, which is Jesus himself. We receive those two again and again.

Confession comes first the way washing your hands comes before dinner. You want a clean heart at God’s table.

The Bible is God speaking through human writers. Prayer is you speaking back. Commandments, sacraments, Scripture, and prayer are four ways to stay close to him.

Saint Ignatius was a proud soldier. After he was wounded, he spent a long time in bed and started to want the life of the saints more than fame. He confessed, studied, and later gathered friends to serve Jesus. A hard season can become a turning point.

Look at your heart

At home

Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession. A parent can say, “I sin too, and I ask God for mercy.” Each evening, write two good choices and two choices you need to tell God. The boxes stay on this phone.

To reconcile means to come back together. When we sin, Jesus does not slam the door. He gives a sacrament so the friendship can be healed.

Your conscience is the inner sense that says, “That was right,” or “That was wrong.” Ask the Holy Spirit to shine a light on your day. Look at the commandments one by one.

The sacrament has six parts. You will practice them in order: look at your heart, meet the priest, tell your sins, receive a penance, pray that you are sorry, and receive absolution.

Saint Augustine wasted years on selfish choices while his mother, Monica, kept praying. A page of Scripture woke him up. He confessed and spent the rest of his life teaching people the mercy he had received.

The six parts

  1. 1. Examination of conscience. Remember the sins you have chosen.
  2. 2. Welcome. The priest greets you and may pray with you.
  3. 3. Confession. You tell the priest your sins.
  4. 4. Penance. The priest gives you a prayer or a kind act.
  5. 5. Act of Contrition. You pray that you are sorry.
  6. 6. Absolution. Through the priest, God forgives your sins.

    The priest welcomes you

    At home

    Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession. After Mass, walk into the empty reconciliation room. Sit in the chair or kneel by the screen so the room is familiar.

    You tell a priest because Jesus gave his apostles that work, and priests share it today. The priest is not taking Jesus’ place as a stranger. He is the way Jesus hears you now.

    He wears a stole, a long cloth around his neck, as a sign of that work. You may sit face to face or kneel behind a screen. Both are real confession.

    He will greet you. Then you begin, “Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. This is my first confession.” Next time you tell him how long it has been.

    The bag of stones on this page is our picture of sin. Each stone is a wrong choice you are carrying. Jesus can take the bag. You do not need to wear one at home.

    Each pebble is a sin we carry. Add some, then give the bag to Jesus.

    The bag is empty.

    7

    Tell the truth

    At home

    Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession.

    Confession is the moment you name your sins. The whole sacrament is bigger than that one moment. In church it is also called the Sacrament of Penance. It is a turning, a healing task, and a reunion.

    Say the kind of sin and about how many times. You do not need a long story. “I lied, about four times. I was mean to my brother, a lot.” If you are not sure, tell it anyway and let the priest help.

    Spilled milk is an accident. Thrown milk, chosen in anger, is a sin. Confess choices, not accidents.

    The priest will not scold you like a punishment. He has promised God never to repeat what you say. Not to your parents. Not to your teacher. Not later in the hallway. Saint John Nepomucene died rather than break that promise. Your confession is between you and God.

    When you are finished, say, “Please forgive me for these sins and any others I cannot remember.”

    Make it better

    At home

    Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession.

    After you tell your sins, the priest does two kind things. He gives you a little advice. Then he gives a penance: a prayer, or a good deed for someone you hurt.

    The penance does not buy forgiveness. Forgiveness is a gift. The penance is how you show you want to repair the harm. Do it as soon as you can.

    Padre Pio spent long hours hearing confessions because he wanted people to tell the whole truth and leave lighter. You do not need a famous priest. Any priest can offer you the same mercy.

    Say you are sorry

    At home

    This is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance. It is the prayer your child says out loud in confession.

    Being sorry is a decision, not a feeling you must perform. You might not cry. You can still mean it. If you know you are not sorry, say that to the priest before the prayer. He will help you.

    The Act of Contrition tells God three things. You regret the sin. You love him. You want help to live differently.

    O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended you, and I detest all my sins because of your just punishments, but most of all because they offend you, my God, who are all good and deserving of all my love.     Absolution is the priest’s prayer that announces God’s forgiveness. He extends his hand and blesses you. Through him, Jesus forgives the sins you confessed. You answer, “Amen.”

    Then do the penance and thank Jesus in your own words. The secret of what you said still stands. The priest does not bring it up later.

      Why we tell a priest

      At home

      Parents: the lessons are in our own words. The only prayer printed for your child to memorize is the Act of Contrition from the Order of Penance, the prayer said in confession.

      On the first Easter night, Jesus came to his frightened friends. He gave them peace, and he gave them the Holy Spirit. He told them that the sins they forgave were forgiven, and the sins they held back were held back.

      He also trusted Peter with the keys of the Kingdom. Bishops carry on that trust. Priests share in it. That is why a serious sin is told to a priest, not only to a friend.

      If we tell God the truth, he is faithful. He forgives and he cleans the heart.

      12

      You do not have to be afraid

      At home

      If you practice at home, a parent may listen to the child’s words. Do not pretend to be the priest, and do not say the prayer of absolution. That prayer belongs to the priest.

      • Name the sin in a few words.
      • Add about how many times, if you can.
      • Do not hide a sin on purpose. A forgotten sin is still forgiven. Mention it next time.
      • Jesus already knows. Confession lets him heal you.
      • You may confess small sins. The Church asks you to confess mortal sins.
      • If you are nervous, say so. The priest will slow down.

      How to go to confession

      These steps match the six parts in Lessons 5 through 8, with the Sign of the Cross and doing your penance added. Practice them.

      1. 1. Look at your heart

        Pray. Think about the commandments. What do you need to tell God?

      2. 2. Go to the priest

        You may kneel behind a screen or sit face to face. The priest is happy you came.

      3. 3. Begin

        Make the Sign of the Cross. Say, “Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. This is my first confession.”

      4. 4. Tell your sins

        Say them simply and honestly. If you forget, the priest will help you.

      5. 5. Listen

        The priest may talk with you. He gives a penance: a prayer or a kind thing to do.

      6. 6. Pray you are sorry

        Pray the Act of Contrition.

      7. 7. Receive forgiveness

        The priest prays the words of absolution. God forgives you. Make the Sign of the Cross.

      8. 8. Do your penance

        Do the prayer or kind act right away if you can. Thank God.
