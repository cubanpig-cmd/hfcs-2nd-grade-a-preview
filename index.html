The Memory Jar

An original practice story we wrote. The packet passage is on paper.

Grandma has a glass jar on her kitchen shelf. It is full of little things. Each thing helps her keep a memory.

One day I asked, “Why do you keep a pinecone?” Grandma smiled. “That is from our walk in the woods. The wind was cold, but we found a deer.”

Next I found a blue button. “That fell off your coat on the first day of school,” she said. “You were brave.”

At the bottom was a dry yellow leaf. Grandma got quiet. “That is from the day Grandpa’s old dog, Max, went to heaven. It was a sad day, but I want to remember him.”

Then Grandma gave me a small jar of my own. That night I put in a shell from the beach. Now I am keeping memories, too.

About 150 words.

Questions for each day

  1. Day 1 · Key details

    What did Grandma keep from the walk in the woods? What did she keep from the sad day?

  2. Day 2 · Point of view

    Who is telling this story? Who is “I”? How do you know?

  3. Day 3 · Picture clues

    Draw a picture of the jar. Use the words in the story to show what is inside. What made one day sad?

  4. Day 4 · Extend it

    If you had a memory jar, what would you put in it first? Tell about the day it helps you remember.

One-minute reading

Each day, read the story for one minute. A grown-up counts mistakes. Write down the words read correctly.

Daily focus questions

1 of 6
Important details

Where do Mia and Leo read?

Grown-ups help fluency at home. Here is what that looks like in kid words.

  1. Step 1

    Read it out loud

    Hearing the words helps your brain. Sit with a grown-up and read the passage with your voice, not just your eyes.

  2. Step 2

    Try it more than once

    The first time you learn the story. The next times you get smoother. That smooth reading is called fluency.

  3. Step 3

    Point as you go

    Slide a finger under each word. It keeps your eyes from jumping ahead and missing a word.

  4. Step 4

    Slow is smart

    If a word is tricky, pause, sound it out, then reread the sentence. Speed comes after you get the words right.

  5. Step 5

    Tell what happened

    After you read, tell someone the story in your own words. That shows you understood, not just said the words.

Catholic learning · October

October faith

October is the month of the Holy Rosary. We also remember St. Thérèse, the Guardian Angels, St. Francis, St. Teresa of Ávila, and Sts. Simon and Jude.

Virtue of the month

The Little Way

Do small things with great love.

  • Pray one Hail Mary for someone you love.
  • Help at home without being asked.
  • Smile at a classmate who is left out.
  • Say a kind word when it is hard.

Our school name

The Holy Family

JesusMaryJoseph

Jesus is the Son of God. Mary is his mother. Joseph cared for them. Our school is named Holy Family because we try to love God and one another the way they did.

Saints and feast days

Oct 1

St. Thérèse

The Little Flower taught us to do small things with great love.

At Mass

  • The Rosary

    One decade is one Our Father, ten Hail Marys, and one Glory Be. We think about a moment in the life of Jesus.

  • Sign of the Cross

    We begin and end prayer this way. It reminds us of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

  • The Lord be with you

    We answer, “And with your spirit.”

  • Quiet hearts

    In church we walk slowly, use quiet voices, and look at the altar.
