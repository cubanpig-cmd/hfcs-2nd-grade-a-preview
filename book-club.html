Week 1

Book Club — Puppy Problems

Puppy Problems (Peanut Butter & Crackers) by Paige Braddock

This week’s reading

  • Pages due: 1–32
  • Pages 1–32 were due before the October 6 meeting.
  • Meeting: Tuesday Book Club was October 6, 2026. The next page range is not posted yet.
  • Bring the book.

Who’s who

From pages 1–32. Their names together sound like the snack.

  • Crackers

    The blue dog. He loves naps, barking, and chasing squirrels off his porch. He goes “crackers” over squirrels.

  • Butter

    The orange-and-white cat. She’s grumpy and funny, and she mostly cares about dinner.

  • Peanut

    The new puppy. He’s little and doesn’t know much yet.

  • The human

    He owns them all. You only ever see his legs and his red sneakers.

What happens

Pages 1–32 only. Later pages are still a surprise.

  1. Crackers is napping when a squirrel steals an acorn from the porch. Crackers doesn’t even like acorns, but he’s upset because the porch is his.
  2. The human comes home with a big box, and there’s a puppy inside. The puppy pees next to Crackers’ water bowl and drinks out of it.
  3. The human sets up a cage for the puppy and then leaves. Crackers worries that the puppy is staying. Butter says, “Don’t know, don’t care.”
  4. Crackers tries to nap, but the puppy cries and cries, so he goes out to the porch.
  5. The human brings the puppy outside. The puppy doesn’t know anything. Crackers has to tell him what stairs and grass are, and then the puppy eats grass and throws it up.
  6. At dinner the puppy gobbles his food and pushes Crackers away from his bowl. “Back off, chow hound!”
  7. They trade names: Peanut, Butter, and Crackers. Peanut doesn’t even know what butter is.
  8. Peanut falls asleep. Crackers says, “Good night and good riddance,” and thinks life is back to normal.
  9. In the middle of the night, Peanut starts howling. Butter tells Crackers it’s his turn to check on him. Crackers groans that he was just there, and Butter jokes that dogs drool.

Page trail

  • Pages 15–19

    The human leaves. The puppy is still in the cage. Butter says Crackers should care. Crackers only perks up when he thinks of the can opener and dinner. The puppy cries “WAAA WAAAA!” Butter goes out the doggy door. “This is much better.”

  • Pages 20–24

    The human sets the puppy on the porch. The puppy asks what the backyard is. Butter explains stairs and tickly grass. The puppy eats grass and throws up. Crackers coughs up a hairball and jokes that the human collects them.

  • Pages 25–30

    Dinner! Crackers races in. Peanut gobbles food and pushes him away. They trade names. Peanut doesn’t know what butter is. Butter says it’s “only the best thing ever.” Peanut falls asleep. Crackers says, “Good night and good riddance.”

  • Pages 31–32

    Peanut howls in the night. Butter says it is Crackers’ turn. Crackers groans. Butter jokes about dogs drooling.

Words to know

  • Needy

    You want a lot of attention and help.

  • Good riddance

    What you say when you’re glad something is gone.

  • Nocturnal

    Awake at night, the way cats are.

  • Clueless

    Not knowing what’s going on.

Talk about it

  1. 1. Why was Crackers upset when the puppy arrived?
  2. 2. Why couldn’t Crackers take his nap, and where did he go?
  3. 3. What new things did Peanut see in the backyard for the first time?
  4. 4. Why is the book called Peanut Butter & Crackers?
  5. 5. Was Crackers being nice to Peanut? How do you think Peanut felt being new and alone at night?
  6. 6. Have you ever been new somewhere? What helped you feel better?

Prediction: Will Crackers and Peanut become friends? What do you think happens next?

Answer key for grown-ups
  1. The puppy peed next to his water bowl, drank out of it, and cried a lot. Crackers was also worried the puppy was staying for good.
  2. Peanut kept crying in his cage, and it was too noisy to sleep. Crackers went out the doggy door to the porch.
  3. He saw the backyard, the stairs, and the grass. The grass tickled him, and he ate some and threw it up.
  4. Those are the three animals’ names. Peanut is the puppy, Butter is the cat, and Crackers is the dog. Together they sound like the snack.
  5. Not really. Crackers called him needy, said “good riddance,” and told him to back off at dinner. Peanut probably felt scared and lonely. Crackers did answer questions in the yard, so he was a little helpful.
  6. No wrong answer. A first day of school, a new class, or meeting new kids all count.
  7. Prediction: no right answer. A good guess is they slowly become friends.
